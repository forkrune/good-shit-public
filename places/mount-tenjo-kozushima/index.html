Mount Tenjō, Kōzushima

神津島・天上山

A compact oceanic volcano whose white rhyolitic basin, crater ponds and exposed sea-facing ridges create an unusually varied nontechnical mountain day.

Why it is worth considering

The research rated this the strongest single under-recognized discovery: a highly varied volcanic hike with an unusually high experience-to-technical-difficulty ratio.

About

Mount Tenjō rises directly above Kōzushima in the Izu Islands. The valuable unit is the summit-plateau journey rather than one lookout: forest approach gives way to pale rhyolitic terrain, ponds, eroded basins and ridges with ocean views in several directions. The standard mountain is accessible to an ordinary prepared hiker, but the plateau is exposed and strong wind or poor visibility can erase much of the payoff.
