Iwami Tatamigaura

石見畳ヶ浦

A broad Miocene wave-cut platform whose repeating nodules, potholes and horizontal beds become especially legible at low tide.

major-detourlow-tideearthquake-upliftwave-cut-platform

Why it is worth considering

Major-detour worthwhile for geology and unusual coastlines if timed for low tide and moderate seas. High water hides much of the pattern, so tide planning is part of the recommendation rather than a minor optimisation.

About

The platform is geologically notable because the 1872 Hamada earthquake raised it roughly 1–1.5 m, exposing a geometric shore surface with calcareous nodules and potholes. Unlike a small outcrop, the feature occupies enough of the coast to read as a landscape. A coastal tunnel adds to the arrival experience.

Location: Iwami Tatamigaura coast · Hamada · Shimane · Japan

Challenge evidence

Limitations
Higher water conceals much of the platform geometry and wet rock can be slippery.
Evidence against visiting
At an unsuitable tide the visually distinctive pattern is substantially reduced, turning a major detour recommendation into a much weaker stop.
